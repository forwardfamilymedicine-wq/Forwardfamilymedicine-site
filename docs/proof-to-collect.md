# Proof to collect — 90-day plan

This is a punch list of proof genuinely **missing** from the site, not a
list of what to write. Testimonials are not the bottleneck — the practice
has 26 real five-star Google reviews and only about a third of them are
placed on the site as of this batch (see "What's already in hand" below).
Nothing in this document should be filled in with invented quotes,
statistics, or media mentions. Where a number is needed, it comes from the
practice's own records or it doesn't get published.

## What's already in hand (not a gap — for context)

- 26 verified five-star Google reviews exist; 8 distinct reviews are placed
  on the site as of the trust-proof batch (homepage, Lifestyle Medicine,
  menopause, Landi bio, Bigley bio). Lieberman's own bio page currently has
  no review block because his three placed reviews are already used
  elsewhere — the next 1-2 reviews pulled from the remaining ~18 should go
  there first.
- One real press citation (Slate, Aug 2026), now surfaced on the homepage
  and the Direct Primary Care page.
- Real board certifications and credentials for all three physicians,
  captured in the providers collection and now surfaced on cards and bio
  heroes, not just bio sidebars.

## Genuine gaps

### 1. A menopause-specific review
Every Landi review currently on the site (E.G., on both `/menopause` and
his bio) is a general family-medicine review — none of the 26 reviews on
file specifically describe his menopause/midlife care. This is the single
most valuable piece of proof to collect next: the menopause page is a
distinct acquisition pathway and currently has to borrow proof that doesn't
match its subject. Ask 2-3 current menopause patients directly, in writing,
for permission to use a short quote about that specific care.

### 2. Physician video
Zero video exists anywhere in the codebase today (checked for `<video>`,
YouTube, Vimeo, Loom embeds — none). A deciding patient reads a lot of text
proof on this site and sees zero motion of the actual physicians. Suggested
formats, roughly in priority order:
- A 60-90 second "meet the doctor" clip per physician (Lieberman, Landi;
  Bigley once useful given his panel is full; Ashley for the operations
  side), same questions each time for consistency: why they practice this
  way, what a first visit feels like, who they're a good fit for.
- Dr. Lieberman explaining Lifestyle Medicine in his own words, embedded on
  `/lifestyle-medicine` — the page's whole argument is that this is
  practiced differently, and video is the format that actually shows it.
- Dr. Landi explaining his MSCP/menopause approach, embedded on
  `/menopause` — this doubles as a menopause-specific proof point even
  before a menopause-specific written review exists (see #1).
- A short "what a Meet & Greet actually looks like" walkthrough, aimed at
  reducing the friction of the first ask.

### 3. Patient-experience review solicitation, organized by theme
The 26 reviews on file weren't collected against a structure, so they
cluster around whichever thing happened to stand out to each patient. A
short question set, asked directly of consenting patients, would let future
review requests fill the specific themes the site's message hierarchy
depends on rather than relying on whatever a patient happens to mention:
- **Relationship** — "What's different about having a doctor who actually
  knows you and your history?"
- **Time** — "Describe a visit where you didn't feel rushed — what did that
  make possible?"
- **Access** — "Tell us about a time you needed to reach your doctor
  quickly. What happened?"
- **Continuity** — "How has seeing the same doctor over time changed your
  care, versus your experience before?"
- **Comprehensive / proactive care** — "Has your doctor ever caught or
  addressed something before it became a bigger problem?"

### 4. Media beyond Slate
One real citation exists. Worth a deliberate, modest pitch effort rather
than passive hope for more — Main Line and Philadelphia-area outlets
(Main Line Today, Philadelphia Business Journal, WHYY) and DPC-trade press
(DPC News, the DPC Coalition's own channels) are the natural fits given the
practice's actual geography and model. This is a pitch list, not a claim —
nothing here should be described on-site as an existing mention until it
is one.

### 5. Office photography
All three location entries in the content collection carry `photos: []` —
there are no interior or exterior photos of any office anywhere in the
site. A patient evaluating "should I go here" has no visual sense of the
physical space before walking in. Straightforward to fix: real photos of
the Wayne office now, West Chester and Malvern once those offices open.

### 6. Employer-side proof for `/for-businesses`
That page currently carries zero patient or client proof of any kind — no
review, no case study, no quote from an employer who has actually enrolled
staff. If any employer relationship exists or is signed, a short
attributed quote from the decision-maker would be the first real proof
that page has ever had.

### 7. Metrics worth tracking now, to cite honestly later
None of these are collected today. Each is a real, internal number the
practice could start tracking immediately so that in 6-12 months it has
verifiable figures to replace the generic, unsourced industry comparisons
that currently appear on `/direct-primary-care`, `/concierge-medicine`,
`/services`, and elsewhere (7-12 minute visits, 2,000-3,000 patient panels,
etc. — see the separate DPC-statistics discovery for the full list).
Candidates, in rough order of how easy they'd be to start capturing:
- Actual current panel size per physician, against the ~250 cap (already
  policy; this would make it a measured fact instead of just a ceiling).
- Actual same-day / next-day appointment fulfillment rate.
- Average message response time.
- Average visit length, logged rather than asserted as a 1-2 hour policy.
- Patient retention rate at 6 months and 1 year.
- Meet & Greet → enrollment conversion rate (useful internally even before
  it's ever published).

None of these should be published as a specific figure until the practice
has actually measured it — an estimated or rounded number presented as
real would be exactly the kind of unsupported claim this whole trust-proof
effort exists to move away from.

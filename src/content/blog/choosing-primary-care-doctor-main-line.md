Finding a primary care physician in Wayne, Malvern, or elsewhere on the Main Line should involve more than finding the closest office or the first available appointment.

Your primary care physician may be the person who helps you interpret a confusing test result, coordinate care between specialists, manage a chronic condition, or identify a health problem before it becomes more serious. The structure of the practice can influence how well that relationship works.

Here are five questions worth asking before choosing a doctor.

## 1. How quickly can I get an appointment?

Ask what happens when you develop a new problem. Are same-day or next-day appointments usually available? Will you see your own physician, or whoever has an opening?

Access matters because some concerns are easier to evaluate while symptoms are developing, rather than weeks later.

## 2. How much time does the physician spend with patients?

Appointment length varies considerably between practices.

A brief appointment can be appropriate for a focused issue. More complex concerns, multiple chronic conditions, and preventive planning may require more time.

Ask how long a typical new-patient appointment lasts and whether follow-up visits allow enough time to discuss your questions.

## 3. Will one physician know my history?

Continuity helps a clinician recognize patterns over time. The same physician can connect changes in symptoms, medication effects, laboratory results, and family circumstances.

Ask how the practice handles messages, test results, referrals, and follow-up after a specialist visit.

## 4. How does the practice approach prevention?

Good preventive care is individualized. It includes appropriate cancer screening, cardiovascular risk assessment, vaccination recommendations, lifestyle counseling, and attention to mental health.

Be cautious of practices that promise to test for everything or imply that expensive testing is necessary for everyone. A good physician explains why a test is appropriate and how the result could change your care.

## 5. How does payment work?

Traditional primary care practices generally bill insurance for covered services. Direct primary care practices instead charge a recurring membership fee for an agreed scope of primary care services, usually without billing insurance for those services.

Some patients prefer traditional insurance-based care. Others value the access, continuity, and longer visits offered by a membership practice.

DPC membership is not comprehensive health insurance. Patients generally need separate coverage for hospitalizations, specialist services, and other care outside the membership.

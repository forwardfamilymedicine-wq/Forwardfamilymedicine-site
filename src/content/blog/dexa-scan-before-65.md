Osteoporosis can develop quietly. Many people do not know their bones have become weaker until a fracture occurs.

A DEXA scan, also called a DXA scan, is a low-radiation imaging test used to measure bone mineral density. Although routine screening is recommended for women beginning at age 65 under current U.S. Preventive Services Task Force guidance, some people benefit from testing earlier.

The decision depends on individual risk, not age alone.

## Who might need a DEXA scan before 65?

Earlier assessment may be appropriate for:

- Postmenopausal women younger than 65 who have risk factors for osteoporosis.
- Adults who have experienced a fragility fracture, such as a fracture following a fall from standing height.
- People with prolonged exposure to certain medications, particularly systemic glucocorticoids.
- Individuals with medical conditions associated with bone loss.
- People with low body weight, a history of significant nutritional deficiency, or other relevant risk factors.

Men and younger adults may also need evaluation in specific clinical circumstances. Screening decisions should reflect the person's history and the applicable clinical guidelines.

## What does a DEXA scan measure?

Clinical bone density testing commonly measures the hip and lumbar spine. Results are reported using T-scores in postmenopausal women and men aged 50 and older, while Z-scores are particularly relevant in younger adults and certain other populations.

A T-score compares bone density with that of a healthy young adult reference population. A result of -2.5 or lower at an appropriate diagnostic site is one criterion used to diagnose osteoporosis in postmenopausal women and men aged 50 and older.

A DEXA result is not the whole story. Fracture history, age, medications, underlying disease, and overall clinical risk also matter.

## Bone density is only one part of bone health

Weight-bearing activity, resistance training, adequate nutrition, sufficient calcium and vitamin D, avoiding smoking, and limiting excessive alcohol can support bone health.

The best plan depends on your age, dietary intake, medical history, and fracture risk. Supplements are not automatically necessary for everyone, and established osteoporosis may require medication in addition to lifestyle measures. ## What about body composition scans?

Some DEXA systems can also estimate body composition, including fat mass and lean mass. These measurements answer different questions from diagnostic bone density testing.

A body-composition scan should not be confused with a clinical osteoporosis assessment, and the appropriate interpretation depends on the test performed and the patient's circumstances.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does everyone need a DEXA scan at age 50?

No. Routine screening is not recommended for every average-risk adult at that age. Some individuals need earlier assessment because of their risk factors.

### Is a DEXA scan safe?

It uses a very low dose of ionizing radiation. The benefits and appropriateness of testing should still be considered for the individual.

### Can exercise improve bone density?

Appropriate resistance and weight-bearing exercise can support bone health and physical function, although the degree of improvement varies and exercise alone is not sufficient treatment for everyone with osteoporosis.

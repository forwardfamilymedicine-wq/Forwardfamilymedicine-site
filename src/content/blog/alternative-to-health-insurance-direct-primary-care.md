Every fall, open enrollment brings an opportunity to reconsider your health coverage. For some individuals and families, that means comparing insurance plans with rising premiums, high deductibles, and out-of-pocket costs that can make routine medical care feel expensive even when you have coverage.

If you are looking for an alternative to traditional health insurance, you may have come across direct primary care, health-sharing arrangements, or other ways to pay for medical care outside the traditional insurance model.

These options are not interchangeable. Understanding what each one does, and what it does not cover, can help you make a more informed decision for yourself and your family.

For people who value a close relationship with their physician, direct primary care may be worth considering.

## What is direct primary care?

Direct primary care, often called DPC, is a healthcare model in which patients pay their primary care practice a recurring membership fee in exchange for access to defined primary care services.

Rather than billing health insurance for covered office visits, a DPC practice receives payment directly from its members. This structure can allow for longer appointments, more direct communication with your physician, and greater continuity of care.

Depending on the practice, membership may include services such as:

- Routine and preventive visits
- Evaluation and management of common illnesses
- Chronic disease management
- Medication management
- Lifestyle and nutrition counseling
- Direct communication with your physician
- Care coordination and guidance when specialty care is needed

Services, pricing, and membership terms vary by practice, so it is important to understand exactly what is included before joining.

## Does direct primary care replace health insurance?

No. Direct primary care is not a substitute for comprehensive health insurance.

A DPC membership generally covers an agreed-upon set of primary care services. It does not necessarily cover emergency department visits, hospitalization, surgery, specialist care, or expensive diagnostic procedures.

For that reason, many people who choose direct primary care maintain health insurance for unexpected or high-cost medical needs while using their DPC membership for everyday primary care.

The two can serve different purposes: health insurance helps protect against significant medical expenses, while direct primary care provides an ongoing relationship with a primary care physician.

If you are considering going without health insurance entirely, it is important to understand the financial risk. A single emergency, hospitalization, or major medical diagnosis can result in substantial costs.

## Why consider direct primary care during open enrollment?

Open enrollment is a natural time to look beyond your monthly premium and consider how you actually use healthcare.

When comparing your options, consider the following questions:

**Can you get an appointment when you need one?**

A health plan may provide coverage, but it does not guarantee timely access to a physician. Consider how long it takes to schedule an appointment and whether you can see the same clinician consistently.

**How much time does your physician spend with you?**

If you have several concerns, manage chronic conditions, or want to take a more proactive approach to your health, appointment length and continuity may matter as much as the number of covered services.

**What do you pay when you need routine care?**

Depending on your insurance plan, deductibles, copayments, and coinsurance may affect the cost of office visits, laboratory testing, and prescriptions. A DPC membership offers a more predictable fee for the services included in the agreement, although additional costs may still apply.

**Are you looking for a more personal approach to primary care?**

Some people prefer a smaller practice where their physician has more time to understand their medical history, lifestyle, priorities, and long-term health goals.

Direct primary care is designed around this type of ongoing physician-patient relationship.

## What are the alternatives to traditional health insurance?

If you are reconsidering your healthcare spending, it helps to distinguish between options that provide insurance coverage and options that provide access to care.

### 1. Direct primary care

You pay a recurring membership fee for defined primary care services. This may be appealing if you want more accessible, relationship-based primary care.

**Important consideration:** DPC alone does not provide comprehensive protection against major medical expenses.

### 2. Marketplace health insurance

Individuals and families may be able to purchase comprehensive health coverage through the federal or applicable state health insurance marketplace. In Pennsylvania, that marketplace is Pennie. Depending on eligibility and household income, financial assistance may be available.

Compare premiums, deductibles, out-of-pocket maximums, provider networks, prescription coverage, and expected healthcare needs rather than choosing based on the monthly premium alone.

### 3. Employer-sponsored health insurance

If coverage is available through your employer or your spouse's employer, compare the available plans and employer contributions. Consider whether the plan provides access to your preferred physicians, hospitals, and specialists.

### 4. Health-sharing arrangements

Health-sharing organizations may allow members to contribute toward eligible medical expenses under the organization's guidelines. However, these arrangements are not the same as health insurance and generally do not provide the same legal protections or guarantees of payment.

Review exclusions, eligibility requirements, payment limitations, and the organization's written guidelines carefully before participating.

### 5. Other limited-benefit or discount products

Some products offer discounts or pay benefits for specific services without providing comprehensive health coverage. Read the terms closely so you understand what is and is not covered.

Before selecting any alternative, consider speaking with a licensed health insurance professional about your options and potential gaps in coverage.

## Can you have direct primary care and health insurance at the same time?

Yes. Many patients choose to combine a direct primary care membership with a separate health insurance plan.

This approach allows patients to use their DPC membership for the primary care services included in their agreement while retaining insurance for eligible services outside the practice.

Our post on [direct primary care and insurance](/blog/direct-primary-care-vs-insurance/) goes further into how the two fit together. Before enrolling, review how the DPC membership works alongside your specific insurance plan, including how laboratory testing, prescriptions, referrals, and outside services are handled. Coverage and payment arrangements vary.

If your main concern is a high deductible, compare the cost of a DPC membership plus insurance with your other available options. Consider both the financial cost and the type of access to care you want.

## Is direct primary care right for you?

Direct primary care may be a good fit if you value:

- A consistent relationship with your primary care physician
- Longer appointments and time to discuss multiple concerns
- More direct communication with your physician
- A proactive approach to prevention and lifestyle
- Transparent membership pricing for included primary care services
- A physician who can help you navigate the broader healthcare system

It may be less suitable for someone who primarily wants a lower monthly healthcare expense without an additional membership fee, or who expects the membership to cover hospital care, specialist visits, and other services outside the primary care agreement.

The right choice depends on your health needs, budget, existing coverage, and tolerance for financial risk.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is direct primary care cheaper than health insurance?

They serve different purposes, so they are not directly interchangeable. A DPC membership may provide predictable pricing for included primary care services, but it does not generally cover the range of expenses covered by comprehensive health insurance. Compare the total cost of your membership, insurance premiums, deductibles, and expected out-of-pocket expenses.

### Can I cancel health insurance and use direct primary care instead?

You can explore different coverage arrangements, but DPC alone does not protect you from major medical expenses. Before canceling a health plan, carefully evaluate the risks and consider speaking with a licensed insurance professional.

### Does direct primary care include specialist visits or hospital care?

Usually not. DPC memberships generally focus on primary care services. Specialist visits, hospitalizations, emergency care, and other outside services may require separate payment or insurance coverage.

### When is open enrollment for 2027 coverage in Pennsylvania?

Individual and family plans in Pennsylvania are sold through Pennie, the state's health insurance marketplace. Pennie's open enrollment for 2027 coverage runs from November 1, 2026, through January 15, 2027, and you need to enroll by December 15 for coverage to begin January 1. Employer plans set their own enrollment windows, and dates can change, so confirm the deadlines that apply to you at pennie.com or with your employer.
